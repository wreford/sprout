Two Numbers Your Project Doesn't Track

metrics evm certainty

SPI, CPI, percent complete — every standard metric scores effort against the plan. None of them scores the forecast. The owner is buying a completion date, and nobody measures whether we actually know it.

Every megaproject manager has watched reports stay green while the job gets later. Quantities move on paths with years of float. The one vendor issue that decides the end date sits untouched. SPI applauds. Earned value was built to measure effort, and on a big job effort is cheap. Knowing the ending is what's scarce.

Measure the fog

At any status date there's a window of plausible finishes — the P10–P90 spread. Its width, and how fast it shrinks, is the most important schedule fact on the project. A month that installed nothing but killed the ambiguity around fuel load made real progress. A month of heroic quantities that left the fog alone made none.

So baseline it. At sanction, commit to a certainty profile alongside the milestone dates: window down to six months by month 18, eight weeks by month 36. A date is wrong the day it's printed. A certainty profile is a promise about how fast the project will learn, and it can be scored.

SCI and CCI

Schedule Certainty Index: planned window width over actual, at the status date. 1.0 on plan, above is ahead — same convention as SPI. Cost Certainty Index: the same ratio on the estimate-at-completion spread. The earned-value vocabulary carries straight over: certainty baseline, certainty earned, certainty variance.

Cross them with SPI and CPI. High SPI, high SCI: on track. High SPI, low SCI: quantities moving, fog not lifting — green and getting later. Low SPI, high SCI: eating risk early, often the healthiest project on the books, and punished by every metric in the standard. Low both: trouble. Today we fire the quadrant-three manager and promote the quadrant-two one.

The anti-gaming rule

The obvious cheat is claiming a narrow window with a straight face, so narrowness alone never scores. Claimed distributions get scored against realized dates with a proper scoring rule — the math weather forecasters live under. Overconfidence costs more than admitted uncertainty, and a tight window that proves wrong torches the index retroactively. Sandbagging and hero-dating both lose.

What certainty is worth

On a nuclear build, every year inside the completion window carries capital at billions per year with gigawatt revenue parked behind it. Lenders price the width of the distribution, not the middle of it, and cost of capital dominates nuclear economics. Contingency — ten to fifteen percent of everything — is the posted price of not knowing. Stack the channels and certainty is worth maybe a fifth of project value. No standard metric looks at it.

The engines exist. Every major project runs a schedule risk analysis, reports the P80 once, and throws the distribution away. Keep the distribution. Promise how fast the fog comes down, then score the promise.
