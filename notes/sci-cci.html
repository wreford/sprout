Two Numbers Your Project Doesn't Track

metrics evm certainty sci cci

SPI and CPI score effort against the plan. Nothing scores the forecast. The owner is buying a date; nobody measures whether we know it.

So reports stay green while the job gets later. Work with years of float moves; the one issue that decides the end date doesn't. On a big job effort is cheap — knowing the ending is scarce.

Measure the fog

At any status date the finish is a P10–P90 window. How fast that window shrinks is the real schedule progress. Baseline it at sanction — six months wide by month 18, eight weeks by month 36 — then score the promise.

SCI and CCI

SCI = planned window width ÷ actual. 1.0 on plan, above is ahead.

CCI = the same ratio on the estimate-at-completion spread.

The earned-value vocabulary carries over: certainty baseline, certainty earned, certainty variance.

The circled corner is the one that kills you: quantities moving, fog not lifting. Today that manager gets promoted, and the one eating risk early gets fired.

The anti-gaming rule

A narrow claim scores nothing by itself. Claimed windows are scored against realized dates, the way weather forecasts are. Overconfidence costs more than admitted doubt. Sandbagging and hero-dating both lose.

What certainty is worth

A year of window width is a year of financed capital with revenue parked behind it. Lenders price the width. Contingency is the posted price of not knowing. Stacked up, that's maybe a fifth of project value — and nothing tracks it.

The engines exist. Every big job runs a schedule risk analysis, reports the P80 once, and throws the distribution away. Keep it. Promise how fast the fog comes down. Score the promise.
