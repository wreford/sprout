Two Numbers Your Project Doesn't Track

metrics evm certainty sci cci

SPI and CPI score effort against the plan. Nothing scores the forecast. The owner is buying a date; nobody measures whether we know it.

So reports stay green while the job gets later. Float moves. The one issue that decides the end date doesn't. On a big job effort is cheap — knowing the ending is scarce.

Measure the fog

The finish is a window, not a date — the P10–P90 spread. How fast that window shrinks is the real schedule progress. Baseline it at sanction — six months wide by month 18, eight weeks by month 36 — then score the promise.

SCI and CCI

SCI = planned window width ÷ actual. 1.0 on plan, above is ahead.

CCI = the same ratio on the cost-at-completion spread.

Same vocabulary as earned value: certainty baseline, certainty earned, certainty variance.

Where projects actually live

The bottom-right is the one that kills you: quantities moving, fog not lifting. Today that manager gets promoted, and the one eating risk early gets fired.

The anti-gaming rule

A narrow claim scores nothing by itself. Distributions are scored against realized dates — forecaster math. Overconfidence costs more than admitted doubt. Sandbagging and hero-dating both lose.

What certainty is worth

Every year of window carries billions in capital with revenue parked behind it. Lenders price the width. Contingency is the posted price of not knowing. Stack it up: maybe a fifth of project value — tracked by nothing.

The engines exist. Every big job runs an SRA, reports the P80 once, and throws the distribution away. Keep it. Promise how fast the fog comes down. Score the promise.
