Everything That Stops Work Is a Constraint

A predecessor, a missing spool, an unsigned permit, a crane booked somewhere else, wind over the lift limit, a room too crowded to work in, a radiation dose budget nearly used up. On most jobs those are tracked in seven different places. They're all the same kind of thing: something that has to be true before work can start.

The critical path method made logic ties the main constraint because that's what computers in the 1960s could handle. Ask a superintendent what stopped work this week and it's rarely a predecessor.

Dates come from the constraints

If everything is a constraint, the schedule dates come from the constraints. Each one has the same information: what it holds up, what clears it, who owns it, and when it expires. A permit and a weather window only differ in how they get cleared.

Keep them in one place and you can ask each morning which ones to clear to free up the most work. I don't know yet if one system can handle all seven kinds well. I do know what it costs to keep them in seven. See Planning From Conditions.
