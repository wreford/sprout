How Long Bad News Takes to Reach the Plan

Every problem has two dates: the day someone on site first saw it, and the day it showed up in the plan. On most jobs the gap is days or weeks, and the whole time, decisions get made off a plan that's wrong.

Nobody tracks the gap, so nobody manages it. Start logging both dates for each new constraint and watch the median week to week.

Where the days go

  • Saying it. Crews that don't talk and people who expect blame keep things to themselves.
  • Writing it down. If recording it takes more than a minute, it waits for Friday. A short video on a phone takes thirty seconds.
  • Getting it into the schedule. The whiteboard knows by 6 a.m. The schedule finds out at the monthly update.
  • Reporting. An unsigned green hides what somebody already knows.

A week of lag usually costs about a week. In front of a closing window it can cost a season, and on a decision you can't undo, the news shows up after you've made it.
