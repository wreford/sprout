The Calendar Fights Back

constraints scheduling

A pour needs three dry days. The heavy-lift crane is on site for two weeks in October. The environmental permit allows in-water work until the fish come back. None of these care about your logic ties.

The scheduling tools file a window as a constraint date and a note in someone's drawer. So the plan treats the calendar as infinite, then acts surprised every November.

A window is a constraint with an expiry — miss it and the price jumps from a delay to a season. The ones with cliffs like that belong on the board every morning. Whether that scales past a whiteboard, still testing.
