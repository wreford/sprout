What It Costs to Ask a Question

Every site has a price for asking a question. Some of it is time: finding who knows, then waiting for them. Some of it is looking like you should already know. When the price is high, people guess, and some of the guesses get poured in concrete.

What drives the price up

  • Nobody obvious to ask. The requirement is in a document the electrician will never see, or the one person who can decide is away and nobody else can sign (delegation).
  • Asking gets you blamed. People stop asking where questions get treated as ignorance. The person who asks awkward ones in meetings gets labelled difficult.
  • Answers come too late. A three-week RFI turnaround teaches crews to stop writing RFIs.

Bring it down

Track how many questions get asked and how long the answers take. A lot of questions is fine. Slow answers are the problem. Give instructions with tolerances so fewer questions are needed, and answer first the ones holding up the most work.
