Brooklyn Bridge: Bad Wire and a Safety Margin

Partway through building the cables of the Brooklyn Bridge, the engineers found out a supplier had been sending wire that failed inspection. The rejected wire had been switched back into the deliveries. A lot of it was already in the cables.

Taking it out wasn't practical. The chief engineer, Washington Roebling, had designed the cables with a large safety margin, so he added extra wire to make up the strength and kept going. The bridge opened in 1883 and still carries traffic.

Roebling ran much of the job from his window after decompression sickness from working in the caissons left him unable to get around. His wife, Emily Warren Roebling, took his instructions to the site and became an expert herself.
