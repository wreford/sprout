Ask What-If Before the Meeting Ends

"What happens if the crane is two weeks late?" On most jobs the answer is "let us look at it and get back to you", and the answer comes the following week, after the decision was already made by default.

If the plan is built from the conditions behind it, a what-if is a quick question: change one condition, see which dates move. The scheduler can answer while everyone's still in the room.

Fast answers change the meeting. People ask more questions and test ideas before they commit, the same way pricing a risk in days ends the argument about its colour.
