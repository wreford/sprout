The Darlington Refurbishment

Ontario Power Generation rebuilt Darlington's four reactors between 2016 and 2026 and finished early and under budget, which almost never happens on a nuclear refurbishment. Most of the reason is what they did before the first unit came offline.

Darlington is a four-unit CANDU station on Lake Ontario, east of Toronto. Partway through their life, CANDU reactors need the fuel channels, feeder pipes and other parts inside the reactor replaced so they can run about another thirty years. That means taking a unit offline for around three years and replacing hundreds of radioactive components in tight spaces, to nuclear quality standards. Earlier refurbishments in Canada had run years late and well over budget.

Darlington's was approved at C$12.8 billion for all four units. Work started on Unit 2 in October 2016.

Before the first unit

OPG spent years planning before any unit came offline. The best-known piece is a full-size mockup of a reactor at the site. Crews rehearsed the work on it, tools were tested on it, and the sequence was worked out on it. Problems found on the mockup cost a fraction of what they would have cost inside a reactor.

One unit at a time

The units were done one after another, with some overlap, instead of all at once. Unit 2 came back in 2020, Unit 3 in 2023, Unit 1 in 2024 and Unit 4 last. Each unit used what was learned on the one before: better tools, a better sequence, and crews who had done the work already. The lessons went into the plans and procedures for the next unit, not into a report. Unit 4 was finished in 968 days, the first Darlington unit done in under a thousand.

The last unit came back into service in 2026. OPG reported the whole project finished about four months early and roughly C$150 million under budget. Pickering, OPG's other large station, is next.
