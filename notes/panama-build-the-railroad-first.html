Panama: Build the Railroad Before You Dig

France started the Panama Canal in 1881 and gave up after years of work, with thousands of workers dead from yellow fever and malaria and a sea-level design the ground wouldn't allow.

The United States took over in 1904. Its chief engineer from 1905, John Stevens, slowed the digging down. He rebuilt the railroad, put up housing, built water and sewer systems, and backed William Gorgas's campaign against the mosquitoes. The plan also switched to locks.

The canal opened in 1914. The lesson still holds: the logistics, health and living conditions of the workforce are the first critical path, not overhead.
