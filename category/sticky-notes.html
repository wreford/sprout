Why does a quiet site worry me?

A crew that jokes around is a crew that talks. The cartoon on the trailer whiteboard about the sticking valve keeps more people away from it than last month's report. If nobody jokes, they're probably not passing on what they know either. People who enjoy the work stick around, too. If you need a break from schedules, there's a small idle game here about growing a lemonade stand into a galactic empire.

What do you do with the person who annoys everyone?

Most difficult people have one habit that's useful in the right job. The one who checks every bolt callout catches the wrong grade before the pour. Move them to where that habit helps instead of trying to train it out of them.

Why do some crews act like they're just passing through?

Maybe because we treat them that way. A shared desk, a chair, Wi-Fi that works and a locker that locks are cheap. Give them to everyone, including the cleaners, yard hands and apprentices. People notice who got a spot and who didn't.

Why do hand-drawn roadmaps stick around so long?

No dates, no logic, no owners, so nobody can prove them wrong. Draw the picture from the schedule instead. Then it changes when the schedule changes, and it can't turn into a second version of the job.

Sticky notes · Sep 13, 2026

Is a short video the best protection against claims?

I think so. A lot of claims come down to what an area looked like on a certain day. Thirty seconds of video from that day settles it. Attach the clip to the item it's about, so it's filed before anyone knows they'll need it.

Sticky notes · Aug 30, 2026

Who signs when the signer is on vacation?

Usually nobody, and the job finds out at the first hold point that stalls. Write it down: who can sign for whom, for what, and until when, before the vacation starts.

Sticky notes · Aug 23, 2026

Why does the consultant's report sound familiar?

Because they spent the first month interviewing your superintendent. Hire consultants for narrow, rare problems, like a forensic metallurgist. For running the work, spend the money on your own people, since they're the ones who live with the result.

Sticky notes · Aug 2, 2026

How many milestones does a job have?

On paper, a couple of hundred. Only three or four matter, like the freeze-up window or first energization. Pick those at the start and protect them. If every date is treated as critical, crews stop believing any of them are.

Sticky notes · Jul 19, 2026

Do small cuts and strains tell you anything?

Not about fatalities. But they repeat for the same reasons: the same knife, the same task, the same corner of the yard, and the fixes are usually cheap. You'll only see the pattern if reporting one takes less than ten seconds.

Sticky notes · Jul 5, 2026

What is the one form everybody fills in?

The timesheet, because it gets them paid. Add one question per task: done, partial or blocked, with a photo if it's blocked. Most field data still gets written on paper and typed in later, and a crew-hour costs more than a phone. Where phones are allowed, start there. On nuclear and other sites that restrict them, use a site tablet or a kiosk at the gate, but still start with the timesheet.
