Why this works

Breathing is the one part of the autonomic nervous system you can steer on purpose. Each pattern here slows your breathing, and slow breathing shifts the body toward its calmer, "rest and digest" side.

Resonance · about 6 breaths a minute strong evidence

Your heart speeds up a little on every in-breath and slows on every out-breath (respiratory sinus arrhythmia). Around six breaths a minute this rhythm lines up with the blood-pressure reflex, and heart-rate variability swings to its largest. Higher HRV is linked with better stress regulation. Turn on the camera pulse to watch your own heart rate rise and fall with the guide.

Lehrer & Gevirtz (2014), Frontiers in Psychology 5:756 · Zaccaro et al. (2018), Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 12:353 · Russo, Santarelli & O'Rourke (2017), Breathe 13(4):298–309

Longer out-breath · Calm 4·6 good evidence

The heart naturally slows during exhalation through vagal activity, so patterns with a longer out-breath lean on the brake pedal.

Cyclic sighing randomized trial

Two inhales through the nose, the second a short top-up, then a long exhale through the mouth. In a month-long randomized study, five minutes a day of cyclic sighing improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation did.

Balban et al. (2023), Cell Reports Medicine 4(1):100895

Why Box and 4·7·8 were dropped our call

Both are popular, but few studies test them directly, and long breath holds can feel effortful. Resonance, the long exhale and the cyclic sigh have the clearest support, so those are what's offered. A session defaults to five minutes, the daily dose used in the cyclic-sighing trial.

Why the obby is built this way design from evidence

No failure screens, timers or lives. Time pressure and harsh failure raise arousal, the opposite of the goal. Falling simply fades you back to your last checkpoint.

A gentle difficulty curve with glades. Flow comes when the challenge sits just above your skill. Stages ramp slowly, and every tenth is a wide resting glade.

Breath bridges. Blue platforms rise and fall with the guide, so the course itself rewards breathing at the paced rhythm.

Soft visuals while you move. Screen warping pauses whenever you move, because conflicting motion cues can cause motion sickness. Psychedelia starts low, since gentle "soft fascination" restores attention better than intense stimulation.

Csikszentmihalyi (1990), Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience · Russoniello, O'Brien & Parks (2009), casual video games and stress, Journal of CyberTherapy & Rehabilitation · Reason & Brand (1975), Motion Sickness · Kaplan (1995), above

Nature, sound and soft attention good evidence

People recover from stress faster while watching natural scenes than urban ones, and faster with nature sounds than with traffic noise. Gently interesting scenery holds attention without effort, which lets directed attention rest.

Ulrich et al. (1991), J. Environmental Psychology 11:201–230 · Alvarsson, Wiens & Nilsson (2010), Int. J. Environ. Res. Public Health 7(3):1036–1046 · Kaplan (1995), J. Environmental Psychology 15:169–182

How the camera reads your pulse

Each heartbeat pushes blood into the skin and changes its color by a fraction of a percent. Stillness averages the skin pixels in the middle of the frame and separates the pulse from lighting changes with the CHROM method. A frequency scan then finds your heart rate. Steady light and a still face give the best reading. It's an estimate, not a medical measurement, and the video never leaves your device.

de Haan & Jeanne (2013), IEEE Trans. Biomedical Engineering 60(10):2878–2886

Go gently

If you feel lightheaded or short of air, return to your natural breath. This is a relaxation aid, not a treatment.
