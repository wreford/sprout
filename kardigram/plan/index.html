03 · evaluate printers

How to judge a printer before it's ours.

  • Cardstock path. It must feed 300 gsm (≈1.2 mm) without curling it. Rear or straight-through feed beats a U-turn path every time.
  • Width. A folded 5×7 prints on a 10×7 sheet. 13″-wide printers do that flat, plus art prints to sell later.
  • Ink per card, not sticker price. A cheap printer with cartridges is an expensive printer. Tanks first, always. Do the math per card before checkout.
  • Borderless. Edge-to-edge art is the whole look. If it can't bleed, it can't kardigram.
  • Pigment vs dye. Dye pops on photo stock; pigment survives water. For mailed cards in envelopes, dye is fine and prettier.
  • The test: take one cotton blank to a store (or order Epson's sample print), print our mandala file, and check blacks, banding, and how it handles the fold line.
04 · card stock

Paper is the product.

Fine cotton the brand

$1.00–1.60 / card CAD

Pre-scored 300 gsm cotton blanks with envelopes (e.g. Red River Aurora Art White 5×7). What "expensive card" feels like in the hand. This is our default.

Good white stock

30–60¢ / card CAD

Bulk 5×7 folded blanks (cutcardstock-style, 80–100 lb). For budget postcards and practice runs. Envelope adds ~10–15¢.

Extras

~40¢ / card CAD

Wax seal ~25–30¢ a stamp, tissue liner pennies, QR label stock pennies. Small costs, most of the unboxing feeling.

  • Rule one: order the sample kit before any bulk box. Every paper looks the same online and completely different under our ink.
  • Rule two: the score line must run with the paper grain, or the fold cracks through the art. Pre-scored blanks solve this for us.

07 · marketing

How people find us.

TikTok process video

Card content is ASMR gold: ink hitting cotton, the fold, the wax dropping, the stamp press, the mailbox thunk. 15–30 seconds, real sound, hook in the first second ("this card is going to a stranger's grandma"). Post the process, not an ad — 3× a week, same time. Reply to every comment with a card.

Free ecards already built

Every free PDF is a flyer that friends send to friends — the footer link does the selling. Push the free door everywhere: bio links, video captions, the QR on paid cards. Free users are next month's paid users.

Local first

London ON angle is the story: local artist add-ons, Wortley & Old East makers, "mailed from Richmond Post." One market-day table with a live wax station earns more trust than a month of ads.

Moments beat months

Nobody wants a card in general; everyone needs one Thursday before a birthday. Own the panic searches: "birthday card mailed for me", "card that arrives this week." Our 3 pm same-day cutoff is the ad.

08 · ideas

The list we'll argue about at dinner.

  • Birthday autopilot. The scheduler already exists — sell a year of birthdays in one sitting: type ten names in January, we handle the rest.
  • Card of the month. A subscription: one great card, sealed and addressed to whoever they choose, every month. Predictable revenue, predictable print runs.
  • Corporate thank-yous. Ten handwritten cards beat a hundred emails. Realtors, dentists, wedding vendors — sell in packs of 25.
  • Keira's line. A kid-drawn card series with the artist's age on the back ("art by Keira, 9"). It will outsell the museums. Signed, obviously.
  • QR mixtapes. Lean into the QR: a playlist, a video message, a photo album. "The card that plays your song" is a TikTok in itself.
  • Holiday drops. Limited runs — 100 numbered Valentine's cards, gone when they're gone. Scarcity makes paper collectible.
